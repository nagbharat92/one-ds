Expression

Hue-blocks for colored cards, callouts, and sections. Each block takes its hue from the palette and its lightness and chroma from the theme, so it stays the same hue while tracking the ground. Shown as real blocks because a swatch cannot show whether the fill, ink, and edge pair correctly.

Expression is for several hues coexisting on one page: categories, callouts, and sections a reader tells apart at a glance. A single hue taking over the whole page is not expression, it is a theme. Set that through groundLightness and accentHue in tokens/theme.json and the whole system moves together.

Gray

A block for content that needs its own plane without taking on a hue. The ink and the edge are drawn from the same family as the fill, so the block reads as one material rather than a rectangle laid over the page.

A nested panel steps down to the soft fill and keeps the same ink.

  • --oneds-color-background-expression-gray-subtle
  • --oneds-color-background-expression-gray-soft
  • --oneds-color-stroke-expression-gray
  • --oneds-color-foreground-expression-gray

Navy

A block for content that needs its own colour: a callout, a category card, a section a reader must tell apart at a glance. Turn the theme dial and navy stays navy while its lightness follows the page.

A nested panel steps down to the soft fill and keeps the same ink.

Blue

Expression is not status and not a chart series. Use it when a whole region of content belongs together and the grouping is easier to see than to explain in words.

Green

The fill is light enough to carry body copy at full length, which is the whole point of a hue-block. If text on it needs a size or weight bump to stay readable, the block is doing the wrong job.

Lime

Every hue shares one saturation ceiling, so no block shouts louder than its neighbours. That shared discipline is what keeps ten colours on one page from reading as ten decisions.

A nested panel steps down to the soft fill and keeps the same ink.

Yellow

Yellow is the hue that usually breaks a palette, because a lightness that suits blue makes yellow glare. Deriving every hue from one perceptual scale is what stops that happening here.

A nested panel steps down to the soft fill and keeps the same ink.

Pumpkin

Pumpkin sits close to the default accent hue, which is a useful test: an expression block should still read as content rather than as a control, because it carries no interaction states.

A red block is not an error. Danger has its own family with its own fixed values, because status must read the same in every theme. Expression red is a category, not a warning.
